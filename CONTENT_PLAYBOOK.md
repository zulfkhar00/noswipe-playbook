# Content playbook

What makes short reels in our niche get watched. The nightly run keeps this up to date from our own stats
(`FORMAT_LAB.md`, `nightly/LEDGER.json`). Reference examples are added by the user only.

Tags: `ours` = shown by our own posts · `ref` = shown by a reference account the user picked · `general` = common platform
knowledge, not yet tested by us. Promote a claim when evidence comes in; drop it when our numbers contradict it.

## Hook (first 1-2 s)
- Our skip rate is 41-59%: about half the viewers leave in the first seconds. The hook matters more than anything else. `ours`
- A plain, relatable first-person situation, readable in under a second, worked ("Across the Alley" part 1: 12.5k). `ours`
- Same body, three openings (Seven Across part 1): the plain situation "A stranger on my train leaves me crossword clues." beat confession and puzzle-first on skip (28% vs 41/45), watch (20 s vs 14/9 s) and views (1.7k vs 250). One test, and it went out first. `ours`
- Views are two-level on our account: a floor of 150-270 and breakouts of 1.2-1.8k. Every repeat of a body (same video, new
  hook) has stayed on the floor; only new bodies broke out. Same-body hook tests may be measuring first publish, not the
  hook (order-reversal test 2026-11-07). `ours`
- First publish is not enough: Seven Across P3's first publish, with the same "A stranger on my train" hook pattern, stayed on
  the floor (156 at 3h, 45% skip). Breakouts show by 3h (430-644 views, ~30% skip). The opening body matters. `ours`
- Narrated second person with the reference accounts' mechanics (Ten Swipes Left) stayed on the floor in both episodes
  (150-363 views, 39-48% skip). On our account, silent two-strangers stories beat it; killed 2026-11-08. `ours`
- Mid-season recap strips lost viewers (Dryer P5: 61% skip); a season opener on a clear device held them. `ours`
- Something moving and a clear tension or question in frame 1; no slow fade-in, no logo, no setup. `general`

## Watch time
- Average watch is 9-20 s on our posts of about 45 s. Shorter cuts and an ending that loops into the start should help. `general`
- Moving the vote from 32 s to 24 s (Seven Across P2) brought our first engine-post comments (0.7%, 1.2k views). `ours`
- With 20 s average watch and the vote at 32 s, Seven Across P1 got 0 comments at 1.7k views: the vote has to land earlier (P2 puts it at 24 s, testing). `ours`

## Spread
- Sends (DM shares) are among the strongest signals for reaching non-followers: make it "send this to the friend who does this". `general`
- Series with a cliffhanger bring people back for the next part. `general`

## Reference: @play.malo (picked by the user, studied 2026-11-04)
"Interactive horror series. You decide who survives." Series "Subway" started 2026-10-30: 13 parts in 6 days
(2-3 a day), 171k followers. Views: part 1 14.9M, part 2 6.8M, part 3 4M, part 13 424k. Part 1: 647k likes,
7k comments. Recordings, frame strips: `~/feedback/outliers/play.malo/` (part1-strip.jpg, part2-strip.jpg: one frame
per second). This is one example of what works, not a template to copy: take the mechanics, not the genre.
- Their parts run 99-138 s. We do NOT copy that length: our hard max is 60 s (render cost); the format's
  existing max (45 s) still applies. Take the mechanics, fit them in our length. `ref`
- Second person: "Imagine you miss the last train home..." The viewer is the character, not watching one. `ref`
- Narrated start to finish (one calm voice), with 2-3 word captions big in the centre. `ref`
- Concrete details as quick inserts that make it real: phone at 3% with no signal, "Next train 05:30" board,
  station sign, a clock hitting 00:00. A new shot every 1-2 s. `ref`
- A mystery device: a taped note "Night shift instructions. Rule 1: never board an empty train", the rest torn
  off. `ref`
- Ends on a clear two-way choice at peak tension (empty train, doors open: get on or stay?). Part 1 loops back
  into its first line. `ref`
- The next part opens "So, did you get on or stay?" and plays BOTH branches, each tagged on screen ("GET ON" /
  "STAY"): one ends badly ("This is the end. Let's rewind."), the other continues to the next choice. Every
  voter gets a payoff, so voting is free. `ref`
- Cheap, repeatable look: one plain grey 3D mannequin with a simple cartoon face, one grey world; making it is
  fast enough for 2-3 parts a day. `ref`
- Compared with ours: we already end on a vote. We lack the narrator, the "you", real stakes, and showing both
  branches in the next part. A dating version could be "Imagine the girl across from you on the train smiles
  at you..." ending on "Say hi, or put your headphones back in?". An option, not an order: voice and silent
  formats are both allowed.

## Reference: @dontopenit.stories (picked by the user, studied 2026-11-04)
"Daily stories. Comment to decide what happens next." 12 posts, 106k followers; reel views 50k-3.5M (most 600k+).
Same mechanics as play.malo, in flat 2D close to our look. Top reel (3.5M views, 155k likes, 14.7k sends, 2.6k
comments): "Imagine you're watching TV on the 23rd floor when the sirens start." Frame strips (1 frame/s):
`~/feedback/outliers/dontopenit.stories/`.
- Second person "you" again, narrated with 2-3 word captions centre screen; muted, dark night palette. `ref`
- A rules device on screen: an emergency alert "Rule 1 stay inside, Rule 2 do not use the stairs, Rule 3 trucks
  at Harbor Plaza dawn day 5". Breaking a rule is the tension. `ref`
- A HUD that drives time: "DAY 1/5 · 8:41 PM · TRUCKS IN 4 DAYS" in a corner, so several nights fit in one part. `ref`
- Cheap repeatable shots: a flat faceless figure, one apartment, a peephole circle onto the corridor, the lift
  door. Few locations reused. `ref`
- Every grid cover is the question ("DO YOU LET HER IN?", "DO YOU GET IN THE ELEVATOR?"); the series ends with
  a "FINALE" part. Comments are votes ("Stayyy", "Go"). `ref`
- Their parts run past 60 s; ours stay within our limit. `ref`
